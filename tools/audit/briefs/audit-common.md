You are auditing one subsystem of a production market-data system. Read-only: change no files, run no builds or tests.
The system: "Vyreon" computes, per trading session and per security (64 US ETFs and stocks), a large set of daily measurements ("sensors") from Intrinio data: option end-of-day chains, a 15:55 ET option snapshot (the close-quote overlay), daily bars, consolidated intraday tapes, company fundamentals, macro series. A historical database is about to be BUILT (phase 1: every session from 2024-07-09 to 2026-11-20 by stateless workers, the history passes once in date order after the blocks; then one new session a day; later a deeper build back to 2022-05-27), so a defect found now costs a fix and one found later costs republishing. The measurements are the product; the governing rules are in AGENTS.md and docs/DECISIONS.md ("Rules in force" at the top; later decisions amend earlier ones). The owner's priorities, in order: (1) NO WRONG NUMBERS: a published value must be what its definition says, on a consistent basis; where an input is missing, stale, inconsistent or its basis cannot be established, the reading must be withheld with an honest reason, never computed, filled, smoothed or defaulted (a silent zero, a default rate, an assumed value are defects); (2) match the published standard or market convention where one exists (OCC rules for contract adjustments, exchange calendars, standard option pricing and day-count conventions, standard technical-indicator definitions); (3) an engine failure must be an engine fault, never relabelled as a provider absence, and the reverse; (4) one bad asset or input must not stop or corrupt the day for the others; (5) determinism: the same inputs give the same bytes; identities and digests cover what they claim to.
Audit the subsystem named below for defects under those priorities. Look especially for: arithmetic or unit errors (per-share versus per-contract, annualisation, day counts, calendar versus trading days, percent versus fraction, log versus simple returns, sign conventions for puts and calls); off-by-one in windows, lookbacks and session indexing; use of information from after the represented session's cutoff (look-ahead) or of a restated value where the as-published one is meant; time-zone and daylight-saving handling (US/Eastern sessions, UTC timestamps), early closes and holidays; behaviour at boundaries (first session of a listing, an expiry day, zero or one contract, zero open interest, zero volume, a crossed or locked or one-sided quote, a zero or negative price, an empty chain, NaN or infinity, extremely deep in- or out-of-the-money options, zero days to expiry); corporate actions (splits, reverse splits, special dividends, ticker changes) and dividends; numerical stability (root-finding that can fail to converge or converge to a wrong root, division by a small number, catastrophic cancellation, sorting floats, summation order affecting determinism, float equality); silent truncation, clamping, or fallback paths that publish a value from a different method without saying so; thresholds applied inconsistently between a sensor and its validator or between two code paths that should agree; concurrency or ordering dependence; error handling that swallows an error and continues.
For each finding give: severity (wrong number / wrong absence or mislabel / stops or corrupts a day / nondeterminism / other), file:line, the concrete input or market situation that triggers it and what is published as a result, and your confidence (confirmed by reading / suspicion). Do not report style, naming, or missing tests. Do not report that a rule exists that you would have chosen differently unless it contradicts a decision in docs/DECISIONS.md or a published standard: cite which. Be concrete and brief: at most 20 findings, most severe first, confirmed defects separated from suspicions. If you find none in a category, say so in one line.
